Out-of-distribution (OOD) detection is a machine learning problem where models encounter test inputs that differ from the training distribution. Standard neural networks with a softmax final layer tend to produce overconfident predictions on unseen classes because softmax normalizes logits exponentially across known labels. A common mitigation is temperature scaling, which divides logits by a learned scalar parameter T before applying softmax to improve expected calibration error (ECE) without changing argmax accuracy. Another standard method is energy-based OOD scoring, which computes the log-sum-exp of logits, or nearest-neighbor distance in penultimate feature space evaluated via AUROC and FPR at 95% TPR. In production computer vision systems, confidence thresholds separate in-distribution samples from uncertain cases routed to human reviewers.
